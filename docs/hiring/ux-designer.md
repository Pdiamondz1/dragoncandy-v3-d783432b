## The manifesto — this is the brief

Read this part twice. Everything else follows from it.

**Our guiding rule: less typing = more margin.**

Every main task should take fewer than ten keystrokes. The order of preference is: **voice, then
camera, then pasting a link, then tapping a button. Typing is the last resort.** The target is a
paid campaign live in under 60 seconds.

**Our users are restaurant owners, not product managers.** They are busy, they are not technical,
and they did not want to learn software today. A screen that is elegant to a designer and confusing
to a restaurant owner has failed.

**Setup must feel like progress, never homework.** Show someone what is possible first. Ask for
what you need second. Guide them to the action third. Never ask a person to configure something
before they understand why it matters.

**Human-driven, AI-assisted.** People make the decisions that matter. Donny does the work in
between. The design should always make it obvious which is which — a user should never wonder
whether a machine just committed them to something.

If you understand those four ideas, you understand the product.

## The first 90 days

**Weeks 1–2 — paid trial.** Audit a defined slice of the product and show us what you found and
what you would do. Paid at full rate.

**Days 1–30 — the audit, alongside the product manager.** Go through every surface, on **both**
screen sizes, for all three user types. Assess it for usability and for consistency against the
existing design system. Write down what is confusing, what is inconsistent, and what is simply
unfinished.

**Start with the overall direction, then move to specific screens.** We want the holistic view
first — this was an explicit request from our board.

Your findings go into the same prioritised backlog as the product manager's.

**Days 30–90.** The developers arrive and start fixing what the audit found. You design the fixes,
review what ships, and start on the screens that need genuine redesign rather than repair.

## How we work

- **Two-week cycles.** Monday planning, Friday demo.
- **Linear** for tickets, synced with GitHub. Every ticket names the user type and the screen size.
- **Written and asynchronous by default.** About three hours of live overlap with the US daily.
- **Done means done:** shipped, checked on phone and desktop, verified live.
- Designs go to developers as Figma plus a written description of the behaviour — including what
  happens while it is loading and when it goes wrong. Those two states are where most of our
  product's rough edges are.

## Why take this job

92 screens, real users, a design system to build on, and an explicit mandate to make it simpler
rather than bigger. You would be our first designer, so the visual identity of the product from

The honest risks: pre-revenue, no launch date, and a product built by an engineer, which means some
of what you find will be rough. That is why the role exists.
