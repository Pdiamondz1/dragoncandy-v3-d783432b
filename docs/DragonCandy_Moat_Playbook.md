# The DragonCandy moat playbook: six layers of defensibility for a bootstrapped AI marketplace

DragonCandy's most powerful moat won't come from any single strategy — it will emerge from the compounding interaction of **proprietary campaign data, two-sided network effects, and deep restaurant workflow integration** that together make the platform increasingly irreplaceable with every transaction. The good news: the most defensible moats for a bootstrapped creator-brand marketplace are built through execution and data accumulation, not capital. The concerning reality: in the age of vibe coding, where **25% of YC's Winter 2025 batch shipped 95% AI-generated codebases**, surface-level features can be cloned in days. DragonCandy must build moats that compound over time — where each completed campaign makes the platform smarter, stickier, and harder to replicate.

This report maps a specific, budget-conscious strategy across six moat dimensions with prioritized actions for the first 90 days, 6–12 months, and 12–36 months. Total estimated first-year investment across all moat strategies: **$55,000–$120,000**, achievable for a bootstrapped team of 5–10 people.

---

## 1. The data flywheel is DragonCandy's strongest long-term moat

Data network effects represent DragonCandy's most defensible competitive advantage because every completed campaign generates proprietary intelligence that competitors cannot replicate without building their own marketplace from scratch. NFX founder James Currier identifies six conditions for true data network effects: data capture must be automatic, product value must increase with more data, a minimum threshold must exist before value emerges, incremental data value must not asymptote quickly, data must be central to product value, and customers must perceive the improvement. DragonCandy's restaurant-creator matching data meets all six conditions.

**The flywheel works like this:** more restaurants post campaigns → more creators complete work → more outcome data accumulates → Donny AI gets smarter at matching → higher campaign success rates → more restaurants trust and return → more data feeds the cycle. Netflix's recommendation engine, which drives **80% of all content viewed** and saves an estimated **$1 billion annually** from reduced churn, demonstrates where this compounding leads. The critical difference is that DragonCandy's data is both hyperlocal and vertical — understanding that a sushi restaurant in Austin needs different content than a BBQ joint in Nashville creates granular intelligence that doesn't asymptote quickly.

**What to collect from day one:** Log every interaction event (searches, profile views, comparisons, booking decisions, rejections). Track the full campaign lifecycle from inquiry through performance metrics. Capture creator performance data per platform, content quality scores, audience demographics, and delivery speed. Record restaurant-specific patterns: which cuisine types respond to which content formats, daypart performance, seasonal trends, and menu item engagement. Store all Donny AI prompt-response pairs as future fine-tuning training data. In Supabase, use JSONB columns for flexible event metadata, timestamp everything for time-series analysis, and implement pgvector for creator embeddings as matching sophistication grows.

**The fine-tuning opportunity is real but premature.** Laurie Voss argues convincingly that 2026 is the year of fine-tuned small models — companies like Airbnb and Cursor already use domain-specific fine-tuned models for different tasks. DragonCandy should start with prompt engineering plus RAG on Claude Sonnet 4, then fine-tune an open-source model (Llama or Mistral) using LoRA when it reaches **1,000–5,000 high-quality campaign examples**, likely within 6–12 months. At that point, a fine-tuned Donny AI that predicts "this creator type + this restaurant type + this content format = high performance" will outperform any competitor using generic LLMs.

**90-day priorities:** Instrument comprehensive event logging in Supabase before launch. Build post-campaign surveys for both sides. Create the first Creator Performance Score algorithm. **6–12 months:** Deploy the first recommendation model, launch "DragonCandy Insights" as proprietary restaurant content benchmarks, experiment with LoRA fine-tuning. **12–36 months:** Deploy fully fine-tuned models. The accumulated dataset of **5,000+ restaurant-creator-content-performance records** becomes a genuine competitive moat. Consider monetizing anonymized benchmarks as industry reports. Estimated cost: **$5,000–$15,000** for initial analytics infrastructure, scaling with engineering time.

## 2. Network effects and marketplace liquidity are existential — everything else is secondary

Simon Rothman of Greylock states it bluntly: "Liquidity isn't the most important thing. It's the only thing." For DragonCandy, achieving minimum viable liquidity in a single metro area matters more than any patent, trademark, or feature. The target ratio is **3:1 to 5:1 creators per restaurant** — roughly 20–30 quality food creators and 5–10 restaurants per launch market to reach a **70%+ search-to-fill rate** (7 out of 10 campaign briefs matched successfully).

DoorDash's city-by-city expansion offers the most relevant playbook. While competitors fought over urban cores, DoorDash focused on suburbs — no competition, more drivers with cars, no traffic issues, higher average order values from families, and better unit economics. By 2020, DoorDash operated in **4,000 towns** while UberEats covered only 500 cities. The operational discipline was key: detailed playbooks for each market launch, "launchers" who did deliveries themselves, and a requirement that every employee (including the CEO) does a delivery monthly. DragonCandy should adopt this approach: pick one mid-sized metro with strong food culture (Austin, Nashville, Portland), dominate a single neighborhood, then expand concentrically.

**Preventing disintermediation** — where restaurants and creators meet on DragonCandy then transact directly — is the marketplace's greatest ongoing threat. The defense is providing more value than the fee costs: payment escrow, content rights management, FTC-compliant disclosure automation, attribution data proving ROI, reputation portability only within the platform, and Donny AI's ongoing campaign optimization. A reasonable non-circumvention clause requiring matches made through DragonCandy to transact on-platform for 12 months provides contractual backup.

The creator-as-distribution model is DragonCandy's highest-ROI growth channel. ShopMy grew revenue **196% to $80 million** in 2025 by turning creators into recruiters — creators receive a **+10% commission bonus for 6 months** on referred creators, and **70% of brands onboard their own creators** who then bring additional brands. LTK's referral-gated entry creates exclusivity and makes existing creators feel ownership. DragonCandy should launch a tiered system (Bronze through Dragon) based on completed campaigns, ratings, and referrals — never follower count — with founding creators getting permanent elevated commission rates as "DragonCandy Pioneers."

**90-day priorities:** Pick one city, manually onboard 20–30 creators and 5–10 restaurants (founder-led, just as DoorDash founders did deliveries themselves). Launch creator referral program with $50–100 bonuses per recruited creator or restaurant. Offer founding restaurants 3 free DragonDash deliveries. Cost: **$5,000–15,000**. **6–12 months:** Expand to 2–3 adjacent cities using a documented playbook. Target 50+ creators and 25+ restaurants per city. Build city-specific landing pages for SEO. **12–36 months:** At roughly 200+ creators and 100+ restaurants per city, organic growth should exceed paid acquisition — the tipping point indicator.

## 3. Ecosystem integration makes switching costs prohibitively high

The restaurant technology landscape reveals a critical opportunity: **76% of restaurant operators see tech as a competitive advantage, but only 13% are satisfied with their current setup**. The typical restaurant runs disconnected systems across POS, reservations, delivery, marketing, reviews, and social media — only 1 in 7 have a fully integrated tech stack. DragonCandy can become the connective tissue.

Toast's ecosystem demonstrates the playbook. With **200+ certified technology partners** processing **16% of all U.S. restaurant card volume**, Toast created lock-in through proprietary hardware ($5,000–$15,000 investment that becomes worthless if you leave), 2–3 year contracts, and an all-in-one platform where every additional module raises switching costs. Shopify's approach is more instructive for DragonCandy's stage: a generous partner economics model where partners keep **100% of revenue until $1 million annually** attracted 16,000+ apps and 700,000+ partners — creating an ecosystem so rich that **migration costs run $3,000–$25,000** and take 6–12 weeks.

DragonCandy should pursue three tiers of integration by priority. **Tier 1 (immediate):** Google Business Profile API for auto-posting creator content and tracking foot traffic, Instagram/Meta Business API for direct publishing and engagement analytics, TikTok for Business API, and Stripe Connect for multi-party payments. These integrations cost **$2,000–$8,000** in engineering time and create immediate workflow dependency. **Tier 2 (6–12 months):** POS integrations with Square (most accessible — requires only 5 active sellers to apply, with approval in 2–4 months and Clover's **70% developer revenue share**) and Toast (most impactful but takes 6–12 months for approval). **Tier 3 (12–36 months):** OpenTable/Resy reservation tracking, DoorDash/Uber Eats order uplift data, and a developer API for third-party tools.

The Chrome extension concept ("Donny AI Browser Companion") represents a medium-priority distribution channel. The AI extension market reached **$2.3 billion** in 2025 with 442 AI extensions surpassing 1,000 users, and Honey's growth to 17–20 million users (acquired by PayPal for **$4 billion**) was driven primarily by YouTube influencer marketing at roughly **$3–5 CPA per install**. An MVP extension with 2–3 killer features — content idea generation on any restaurant website, competitive content analysis, one-click campaign requests — could reach 5,000–10,000 installs within 6 months at a build cost of **$10,000–$20,000**.

**Creator lock-in requires building value, not walls.** The most powerful retention mechanisms are portfolio hosting (where campaign results and ratings accumulate into an irreplaceable professional resume), analytics dashboards showing performance benchmarks ("You're in the top 15% of food creators in Austin"), Stripe Connect payment processing with 1099 tax document generation, and AI-personalized coaching via Donny AI. A three-tier certification program — Dragon Scout (3 campaigns completed), Dragon Knight (10+ campaigns with 4.5+ rating plus coursework), and Dragon Master (50+ campaigns with mentorship role) — creates aspiration, quality signaling, and social lock-in modeled on HubSpot Academy and the Shopify Expert program.

## 4. Legal protections cost less than founders think and matter more than they expect

DragonCandy's legal moat strategy should follow a clear priority sequence: trademarks first (immediate), trade secrets second (week 1–4), regulatory compliance third (month 1–3), contracts fourth (month 1–3), provisional patents fifth (month 2–3), and full patents last (month 6–12). Total first-year legal budget: **$31,500–$56,000**.

**File trademarks this week.** The unified USPTO filing fee is **$350 per class per mark** as of January 2025. DragonCandy should file "DragonCandy," "Donny AI," and "DragonDash" in Class 42 (SaaS/AI services) and Class 35 (marketplace/advertising services). With attorney fees of $1,000–$2,500 per mark and a $500–$1,500 prior art search, total trademark investment runs **$4,000–$8,000** for three marks across two classes. Begin using the ™ symbol immediately on all marks — no registration required for common law rights. The registration timeline is 6–12 months, with 84% of intent-to-use applications failing because applicants never file the Statement of Use.

**Trade secrets should protect the crown jewels.** Multiple authoritative sources confirm that trade secrets are outpacing patents as the preferred IP strategy for AI companies — federal trade secret cases hit an all-time high of **1,500+ in 2025**. DragonCandy's AI matching algorithm, scoring methodology, system prompts for Claude Sonnet 4, RAG pipeline configuration, fine-tuning data, and "negative know-how" (what approaches failed) all qualify for trade secret protection. The requirements are straightforward: information not generally known, derives economic value from secrecy, and reasonable measures maintain that secrecy. Implementing NDAs, employee IP assignment agreements, access controls, and a trade secret inventory costs **$3,500–$10,000** in attorney fees and is the single highest-ROI legal investment.

The hybrid approach makes the most sense: patent publicly visible innovations (the campaign-from-URL method, the system-level integration) while keeping the core algorithm weights, prompt chains, scoring methodology, and training data as trade secrets. Google protects its search algorithm as a trade secret precisely because disclosure would enable manipulation.

**Provisional patents provide 12 months of "patent pending" at minimal cost.** USPTO filing is just **$65 for micro entities** (DragonCandy qualifies — fewer than 500 employees, income under ~$251,190). With attorney preparation, two provisional patents covering the campaign-from-URL system and the AI-scored creator matching algorithm cost **$2,000–$5,000 total**. The November 2025 USPTO guidance on AI-assisted inventions is favorable — AI is now analogized to "traditional equipment," meaning humans using AI tools retain full inventorship rights. Focus claims on the specific data flow: URL input → page parsing → entity extraction → campaign parameter generation → creator scoring → brief output. This pipeline involves concrete technical steps, not abstract ideas, strengthening patentability under the Alice framework.

## 5. Regulatory compliance as a genuine competitive advantage

**This is DragonCandy's most underrated moat opportunity.** The FTC's influencer disclosure rules require "clear, conspicuous, and unavoidable" disclosures, with penalties up to **$51,744 per violation**. Brands and platforms share liability for creator compliance. Meanwhile, **73 new AI-related laws** were adopted across 27 states in 2025, with California's AI Transparency Act taking effect August 2026 and Colorado's comprehensive AI Act effective June 2026.

Building compliance into the platform architecture creates three advantages simultaneously. First, it provides a powerful sales pitch to risk-averse restaurant brands: "the only fully FTC-compliant creator marketplace." Second, it creates a genuine barrier for new entrants who must retrofit compliance — as Stripe demonstrated by building PCI compliance and money transmission licensing from day one. Third, it builds trust with both sides of the marketplace at a time when **only 42% of consumers believe AI provides more benefit than harm** (versus 82% of marketers — a 40-point perception gap).

Specific compliance features to build before launch: auto-disclosure tagging that inserts compliant FTC language into platform-generated content, AI content labeling for any Donny AI-assisted material, a compliance audit trail maintaining records of all campaigns, payments, and disclosures, and creator acknowledgment flows requiring disclosure training before campaign participation. Implementation cost: **$5,000–$8,000** initially, mostly engineering time.

## 6. DragonDash as the verb, DragonCandy as the ecosystem

Brand verbification requires five conditions: single-purpose clarity, phonetic simplicity (1–2 syllables ideal), category dominance, natural grammatical fit, and replacing a sentence with a single word. **"DragonDash" is significantly more verb-able than "DragonCandy"** — "Dash" implies speed and action, it naturally shortens to "Just DragonDash it," and it directly communicates the rush delivery value proposition. "DragonCandy" works as the ecosystem brand (the place), while "DragonDashing" becomes the action. Seed the verb in every marketing touchpoint, creator post, and email subject line from day one: "DragonDash your grand opening." The hashtag #DragonDashed should accompany every piece of delivered content.

Positioning Donny AI requires navigating the AI trust gap carefully. Research shows **93.4% of consumers prefer human interaction** for complex tasks, but **62% prefer AI for straightforward requests**. Donny should be positioned as an assistant ("Donny helps you find the perfect creator") rather than an autonomous agent ("Donny finds your creator"). Leading with the Anthropic/Claude pedigree borrows trust from a brand known for safety research. Publishing monthly accuracy reports, showing confidence scores on recommendations, and always providing a human fallback option follows the playbook that Salesforce and Adobe have proven effective.

The Airbnb early-stage branding playbook is most relevant for DragonCandy's first 90 days: founder-led sales visiting 50+ restaurants personally, professional photography of first content deliveries (Airbnb's decision to photograph host homes was the cheapest transformative brand decision in marketplace history), a "founding partner" charter for early adopters creating exclusive status, and identifying the "High-Expectation Customer" on both sides. For restaurants: tech-forward independent owners with 1–3 locations frustrated by agency costs. For creators: local food/lifestyle creators with **5K–50K followers** seeking reliable paid work.

## The 90-day battle plan that builds all six moats simultaneously

The following actions, executed in parallel by a 5–10 person team within the first 90 days, activate compounding defenses across every moat dimension:

- **Week 1–2:** File three trademarks ($4,000–$8,000). Execute NDAs and IP assignments with all team members ($3,500–$5,000). Begin using ™ on all marks. Implement comprehensive event logging in Supabase before launch.
- **Week 2–4:** Launch in one city with 20–30 manually recruited creators and 5–10 restaurants. Activate creator referral program ($50–100 per referral). Offer 3 free DragonDash deliveries to founding restaurants. Build FTC auto-disclosure compliance into platform.
- **Month 2:** File 2 provisional patents covering campaign-from-URL and AI matching systems ($2,000–$5,000). Draft creator and restaurant agreement templates with non-solicitation and tiered exclusivity provisions ($7,000–$12,000). Launch Dragon Scout certification (3 campaigns = badge). Begin Square POS integration development.
- **Month 3:** Submit Toast partnership application (long approval timeline). Launch MVP Chrome extension on Product Hunt. Publish first "Donny AI Transparency Report" showing match success rates. Generate first 10 case studies with real campaign ROI data. Track search-to-fill rate, creator utilization rate, and time-to-fill daily.

**Total 90-day budget: $30,000–$55,000** across legal, marketing, and engineering — leaving a comfortable runway for a bootstrapped company while establishing defensibility across all six moat dimensions.

## Conclusion: the moats that matter most are earned, not bought

The honest assessment for a pre-revenue bootstrapped startup: **patents are insurance and ammunition, but network effects and data flywheels are the real castle walls.** DragonCandy's ultimate defensibility comes from three compounding forces. First, the data flywheel — each completed campaign makes Donny AI measurably smarter at matching in ways a competitor starting from zero cannot replicate. Second, two-sided liquidity — once critical mass exists in a metro area, the chicken-and-egg problem protects the incumbent. Third, workflow integration — when DragonCandy processes payments, generates tax documents, stores content libraries, tracks attribution to POS data, and automates FTC compliance, the switching cost exceeds the value of any alternative.

The vibe-coding threat is real for surface features but irrelevant for these deeper moats. A competitor can clone DragonCandy's UI in an afternoon, but they cannot clone thousands of completed campaigns' worth of matching intelligence, relationships between creators and restaurants, or integrations with POS systems that took months to certify. The **99% reliability rule** applies: reaching production-grade quality for a system handling payments, contracts, compliance, and multi-platform content delivery takes 10–100x the effort of a functional MVP. That gap is DragonCandy's moat in year one — the data and network effects compound to make it permanent.
