## The one thing to say before any number

DragonCandy has **zero paying customers** and Stripe is in test mode. Prices and the
built platform are real and measured; the volumes they are multiplied by are modelled.
Every table below says which is which, and saying it first is stronger than being
asked.

## Slide 10 — Hoboken liquidity

Liquid means **≥ 3 applicants per campaign** over the 14 days it stays open, **and ≥ 5 campaigns open** for a creator to choose from.

At 2 restaurants and 8 creators a month: **month 3**.
At 2 restaurants and only 4 creators a month: **never liquid, at any restaurant count**.

**"What if you are wrong?"** — the honest weak point: at the target ratio we clear the applicant threshold by 6% — 3.2 against a floor of 3.0 — and both inputs are modelled. Volunteer that. The defensible part is that the model can express a creator shortage at all, which is the failure mode that actually kills local marketplaces.

## Slide 13 — the fine-tune claim

**"Why is 1,000–5,000 the magic number?"** It is the right challenge, and the answer is
that the number was defensible but the **unit** was wrong. A campaign is not one
training example: it is a chain that yields a brief (intent), several applicants
resolving to one hire (a preference pair), an approve-or-reject (a quality label) and a
performance record (an outcome). A few thousand campaigns therefore produce tens of
thousands of labelled preference pairs, which is the regime LoRA is sample-efficient
in, at $50–300 a run. Nothing has been fine-tuned yet.

## Glossary — say the plain version first

| Term | Say this |
|---|---|
| CAC | what it costs us to win one customer |
| LTV | what one customer is worth over their whole life with us |
| EBITDA | profit before interest, tax and accounting write-downs |
| GMV | the total value of campaigns run through us, most of which goes to creators |
| gross margin | what is left of revenue after the direct cost of delivering it |
| take rate | our cut of each campaign |
| payback | how many months until a customer has repaid what we spent winning them |
| SAFE | a standard pre-seed instrument that converts to equity at the next priced round |
| valuation cap | the ceiling price this money converts at later |
| MFN | if a later investor gets better terms, this one gets them too |
| churn | the share of customers who leave each month |
| liquidity | enough of both sides that neither is left waiting |
| LoRA | a cheap way to specialise an existing AI model rather than train a new one |
| ARR | revenue restated as a yearly run rate |
