# Go-to-Market — DragonCandy

## 1. Market Context

The creator economy is valued at over $100 billion, but the vast majority of that value flows to top-tier influencers working with national brands. Local businesses — restaurants, cafes, food trucks — are almost entirely left out. They need social media content to compete, but the options are bad: agencies charge $2K–5K/month, Fiverr is a race to the bottom, and DMing creators on Instagram is a coin flip. Meanwhile, there are millions of talented micro-creators (1K–50K followers) who can't monetize consistently because there's no structured marketplace for local content gigs.

The timing is right for three reasons. First, short-form video (TikTok, Reels) has made authentic, creator-shot content outperform polished agency content — a major advantage for DragonCandy's model. Second, AI has matured enough to automate the manual work (briefs, matching, analytics) that made local content marketplaces impractical before. Third, restaurants are emerging from years of digital acceleration forced by COVID — they know social media matters now, but most still don't know how to do it.

The opportunity in the launch city alone is significant. Even a mid-size city has 2,000+ restaurants, most posting zero or low-quality content. If DragonCandy captures just 5% of those restaurants at $200/month average spend, that's $200K/month in platform GMV and $30K/month in platform fees — enough to fund expansion to the next city.

## 2. Launch Strategy

**Pre-Launch (Weeks -8 to -1): Build Supply, Build Buzz**

The pre-launch phase focuses on two objectives: recruiting the initial 50 creators and building anticipation on social media. Supply must exist before demand — no business should sign up and find zero creators available. During this phase, Dame builds in public on Instagram, TikTok, and X, showing the platform being built, sharing the vision, and attracting creators who want early access.

**Soft Launch (Week 1-2): Controlled Beta with 10 Businesses**

Hand-pick 10 restaurants that represent the ideal customer: active on social media (or wanting to be), open to trying new tools, and located within the initial creator coverage area. Offer the first campaign free — the goal isn't revenue, it's proof that the loop works. Complete 10 campaigns end-to-end: brief → match → content → approval → payment. Document everything for case studies.

**Public Launch (Week 3+): Open the Doors**

Remove access restrictions. Launch publicly with social media push, creator referrals, and the case studies from soft launch. Target: 50 creators and 10 paying businesses by day 90. The soft launch case studies become the primary marketing asset — real content, real restaurants, real results.

## 3. Pre-Launch Playbook

**Week -8 to -7: Foundation**

Set up DragonCandy accounts on Instagram, TikTok, and X/Twitter. Establish the brand voice from day one — confident, creative, and direct. Post the "build in public" origin story: why DragonCandy exists, what problem it solves, and what's being built. Target: 3 posts/week on Instagram, 3 on TikTok, daily on X. Content at this stage: screenshots of the app being built, behind-the-scenes development, the marketplace vision explained in 60-second clips.

Follow and engage with local food creators (100+ accounts), local restaurant accounts, and food blogger communities. Don't pitch — just engage authentically. Comment on their content, share their posts, build real relationships.

**Week -6 to -5: Creator Recruitment**

Launch the "Founding Creators" program. Post a creator recruitment video on TikTok and Instagram: "We're building a platform where food content creators get paid $100–300 per gig — delivered to your inbox, no pitching required. First 50 creators get priority matching and guaranteed earnings for their first month." Link to a simple signup form (Google Form or Typeform — the app doesn't need to be ready yet).

Direct outreach to 50 local food/lifestyle creators via Instagram DM. Personalize each message: reference specific content they've made, explain the platform, invite them to be a founding creator. Aim for a 30% conversion rate (15+ signups from DMs).

Share creator success stories from other platforms to paint the picture of what DragonCandy enables. Post content breaking down "how much food creators actually earn" to attract attention.

**Week -4 to -3: Restaurant Outreach**

Begin reaching out to target restaurants. Identify 30 restaurants in the launch city that are active on social media or have expressed frustration about it. Visit in person where possible — restaurant owners respond better to face-to-face conversations. Pitch: "We'll get you professional social media content for a fraction of what an agency charges. Here's how it works in 60 seconds."

Create a one-pager (digital + printable) with the DragonCandy value proposition for restaurants. Include: how it works in 3 steps, sample content from test campaigns (if available) or mock-ups, pricing overview, and a QR code to the signup.

Post restaurant-focused content on social media: "Why your restaurant needs better social media content" explainer videos, statistics about social media driving restaurant discovery, before/after content transformations.

**Week -2 to -1: Final Prep**

Run 2–3 test campaigns with willing restaurants and recruited creators. Document the entire process — film behind-the-scenes of the creator shooting content, show the AI brief generation, capture the restaurant owner's reaction to the delivered content. These become launch content gold.

Send "launch is coming" teasers to the creator waitlist and interested restaurants. Build anticipation. Finalize any app bugs found during test campaigns. Prepare launch week content calendar.

## 4. Launch Week Plan

**Monday — Announce on Social Media**

Post the launch announcement on all platforms simultaneously. Instagram: carousel post with 5 slides (the problem, the solution, how it works, early results, CTA). TikTok: 60-second launch video showing a real campaign from brief to delivered content. X: thread breaking down the marketplace model and why it's different. Include real numbers from test campaigns: "We delivered professional content to [Restaurant Name] in 18 hours. The creator earned $200. The restaurant got their best Instagram post ever."

**Tuesday — Creator Stories**

Feature 3 founding creators: their background, their content, what they're earning on DragonCandy. Post their content and tag them. Let them re-share. This serves double duty: social proof for new creators AND shows restaurants the quality of content they'll receive.

**Wednesday — Restaurant Case Study**

Post the full case study from a soft launch restaurant. Show: their social media before DragonCandy (struggling), the DragonCandy campaign process, the delivered content, and their reaction. If possible, include a quote from the owner. Post as a long-form video on TikTok/Reels and a carousel on Instagram.

**Thursday — Behind the AI**

Show off the AI features that make DragonCandy unique. Screen recording of the brief generator in action: restaurant types a sentence, AI generates a complete brief. Show the matching algorithm finding the perfect creator. This is the "whoa" content that gets shares from tech and marketing audiences.

**Friday — Creator Earnings Milestone**

If any creator has hit a meaningful earnings milestone during launch week, celebrate it publicly (with their permission). Post their payout notification screenshot or create a celebration graphic. "Marcus earned $350 in his first week on DragonCandy." This is the content that makes other creators sign up.

**Throughout the week:** Engage with every comment and DM. Respond within 1 hour during launch week. Every comment is a potential signup. Share any user-generated content about DragonCandy immediately.

## 5. Post-Launch Growth

**Weeks 1-4: Solidify the Foundation**

Focus on campaign completion rate and creator retention above all else. Every completed campaign is a potential case study and referral source. Follow up with every restaurant after their first campaign — ask for feedback, testimonials, and referrals. Follow up with every creator after their first gig — ask what worked and what didn't. Fix the most common friction points immediately.

Continue social media posting at 3x/week per platform. Content shifts from "we launched" to "look what our creators are making." Feature one creator or restaurant story per week. Share actual metrics transparently: number of campaigns completed, average delivery time, creator earnings.

Launch the creator referral program: $25 bonus for referrer and referee on first completed gig. This turns happy creators into a recruiting channel.

**Weeks 5-8: Accelerate Supply and Demand**

If creator supply is the bottleneck: run targeted recruitment campaigns on TikTok and Instagram. Host a "DragonCandy Creator Meetup" — pizza, portfolio reviews, tips for shooting better food content. Film the event for content.

If business demand is the bottleneck: increase restaurant outreach to 10 new restaurants per week. Offer a "first campaign free" promotion. Partner with a local restaurant association or food blogger group for credibility.

If both are growing: start introducing brand sponsorship conversations. Reach out to 5 regional food/beverage brands (hot sauce, local brewery, coffee roaster) that distribute through restaurants. Pitch the sponsored campaign model with soft launch case studies as proof.

**Weeks 9-12: Optimize and Prepare for Scale**

Analyze which acquisition channels drive the highest-quality users (creators who complete gigs, restaurants who come back). Double down on what's working, cut what isn't. Begin preparing playbook for city #2 — document everything that worked in launch city.

Aim to hit 90-day targets: 50 active creators, 10 paying businesses, 10 completed campaigns per week, $10K+ in platform GMV.

## 6. Channel Strategy

**Channel 1: Instagram (highest ROI)**
DragonCandy is a visual product serving visual creators and food businesses — Instagram is the natural home. Post 3x/week: creator spotlights, campaign case studies, before/after content transformations, AI feature demos. Use Stories for daily behind-the-scenes. Use Reels for 60-second explainers and creator content showcases. Expected effort: 4-5 hours/week. Expected return: primary organic acquisition channel for both creators and restaurants. Timeline: results from week 2.

**Channel 2: TikTok (high ROI, high virality potential)**
Short-form video reaches creators where they already are. Content: "day in the life of a DragonCandy creator" format, AI brief generation demos (satisfying to watch), restaurant transformation stories. Trend-jack food-related content trends with DragonCandy twist. Expected effort: 3-4 hours/week. Expected return: highest viral potential, primary creator acquisition. Timeline: unpredictable — one viral video can generate 100+ signups.

**Channel 3: Direct outreach (high conversion, low scale)**
Personal DMs to creators and in-person visits to restaurants. Highest conversion rate but doesn't scale. Use for first 50 creators and first 10 restaurants, then transition to inbound. Expected effort: 5-6 hours/week during pre-launch and first month. Expected return: 30% conversion rate on creator DMs, 20% on restaurant visits. Timeline: immediate.

**Channel 4: X/Twitter (community building)**
Build in public threads about building DragonCandy. Share technical insights, marketplace learnings, and growth metrics. Engage with creator economy, SaaS, and restaurant industry conversations. Less visual but great for credibility and investor/press awareness. Expected effort: 2-3 hours/week. Expected return: credibility, industry connections, potential press coverage. Timeline: slow build, compounds over 3-6 months.

**Channel 5: Creator referrals (scalable after critical mass)**
Once 20+ active creators are earning on the platform, referrals become the primary growth engine. $25 referral bonus is cheaper than any paid acquisition channel. Expected effort: minimal (build the feature, creators do the work). Expected return: projected 30% of new creator signups after month 2. Timeline: kicks in after month 1.

## 7. Content Strategy

The content strategy centers on three pillars: show the product working, celebrate creator success, and educate restaurant owners.

**Show the product working (2x/week):** Screen recordings, walkthroughs, and demos of AI brief generation, creator matching, and content delivery. People need to see that the product is real and functional. These posts target both creators ("see how easy it is to get gigs") and restaurants ("see how easy it is to get content").

**Celebrate creator success (2x/week):** Feature one creator per week with their story, their content, and their earnings (with permission). Creator spotlights serve triple duty: they make featured creators feel valued (retention), they attract new creators (acquisition), and they show restaurants the quality of available talent (demand generation).

**Educate restaurant owners (1x/week):** Posts about why social media matters for restaurants, how professional content drives foot traffic, what good restaurant content looks like vs. bad. These target restaurant owners who are on social media but aren't using it well — DragonCandy's exact customer. Use before/after examples.

**Format priority:** Video-first (Reels/TikTok) for all three pillars. Carousels on Instagram for educational content. Threads on X for technical/marketplace insights. Stories for daily behind-the-scenes.

## 8. Community Strategy

**Where the audience gathers:**

Creators: Instagram (food content creator community), TikTok (creator economy conversation), Facebook Groups for local content creators, Reddit r/ContentCreation and r/Instagram. Restaurant owners: local restaurant Facebook groups, restaurant owner associations, Yelp for Business community, local Chamber of Commerce events.

**How to show up authentically:**

In creator communities: share practical tips about food photography, lighting, and editing — not DragonCandy pitches. Be helpful first. When people ask "how do you find clients?" or "how do you monetize content?", that's the natural moment to mention DragonCandy.

In restaurant communities: share social media tips tailored to restaurants — best posting times, caption ideas, how to photograph food. Again, be helpful first. When owners express frustration with content creation, introduce DragonCandy as a solution.

**Community building on DragonCandy's own channels:**

Create a "DragonCandy Creators" Instagram highlight featuring all creator spotlights. Start a private group chat (Instagram broadcast channel or Discord) for founding creators — early access to new features, direct feedback channel, exclusive tips. This inner circle becomes the most loyal advocates and the feedback loop for product improvement.

Host monthly virtual "office hours" where creators can ask questions, share work, and connect. 30 minutes, low-key, builds relationship and retention.

## 10. Budget Considerations

DragonCandy's GTM budget for the first 90 days should be minimal — the constraint is Dame's time, not money. Here's a realistic budget:

**$0/month: Social media content creation.** Dame creates all marketing content using the same phone + editing tools creators use. This is intentional — it demonstrates that great content doesn't require an agency.

**$0/month: Direct outreach.** DMs, in-person restaurant visits, and community engagement cost time, not money.

**$25/month: Tools.** Canva Pro for quick graphics, or free alternatives. Everything else uses free tiers.

**$100-200/month: Creator seeding.** Fund 2-3 "showcase campaigns" per month during soft launch where DragonCandy pays the creator directly. These generate case study content and prove the model works. Budget: $100-200/month for the first 2 months.

**$25/referral: Creator referral bonuses.** Budget $250-500/month starting month 2 for creator referral payouts. This scales with growth — you only pay when it works.

**$0/month: Infrastructure.** Supabase Pro ($25), AI API (~$50), Lovable hosting (~$20) are product costs, not GTM costs, covered separately.

**Total GTM budget: $150-500/month** for the first 3 months. The most expensive asset is Dame's time — plan for 15-20 hours/week on GTM activities alongside product development.

**Where to invest first if budget increases:** If revenue starts flowing and Dame wants to accelerate growth, the highest-ROI investments in order are: (1) increase creator seeding budget to $500/month, (2) sponsor a local food festival or event ($200-500 one-time) for brand visibility, (3) small Instagram ad budget ($200/month) targeting restaurant owners in the launch city.

## 11. Risks

**Chicken-and-egg stall:** The marketplace can't function without both creators and businesses. If creator recruitment stalls, businesses will sign up, find no matches, and churn. Mitigation: recruit 50 creators before opening to businesses. Guarantee first-month earnings for founding creators to reduce sign-up friction.

**Content quality variance:** Early creators may produce inconsistent quality, damaging business trust. One bad experience could lose a restaurant forever. Mitigation: vet all creators via portfolio review during onboarding. Run showcase campaigns to establish quality benchmarks. Implement AI quality scoring early.

**Restaurant owner apathy:** Many restaurant owners are tech-fatigued and won't try "yet another app." Mitigation: reduce friction to near-zero. First campaign free. Approval in one tap. In-person onboarding for early restaurants. Show them before/after content transformations from their specific cuisine/style.

**Creator payment delays:** Any delay in payment — even once — erodes the core trust proposition. Mitigation: test Stripe Connect payment flows exhaustively before launch. Have a manual payment backup for the first month. Over-communicate payment timelines.

**Platform dependency (Lovable.dev, Supabase):** Heavy reliance on third-party platforms creates risk if pricing changes or services degrade. Mitigation: the codebase is standard React/TypeScript and PostgreSQL — portable to other hosting/backend providers if needed. Keep the architecture decoupled.

**Competition from incumbent platforms:** Fiverr, Upwork, or a new entrant could launch a local content feature. Mitigation: move fast, build deep local network effects, and make the creator-brand-business three-sided model a defensible moat that's hard to replicate.

**Scope creep:** With so many features planned across phases, there's a risk of trying to build too much before validating the core loop. Mitigation: stick to the phased roadmap ruthlessly. Phases 0-4 deliver the core loop. Everything else is iteration. Don't start Phase 5+ until the core loop is proven with real users and real revenue.

**Burnout:** Dame is a solo founder building, marketing, and selling simultaneously. 90-day sprints are exhausting. Mitigation: set realistic weekly goals (not daily). Batch similar tasks (all social media content in one session, all outreach in another). Use AI tools aggressively to reduce manual work. Celebrate small wins.
