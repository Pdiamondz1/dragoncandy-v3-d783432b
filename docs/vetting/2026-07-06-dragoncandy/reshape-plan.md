# DragonCandy — RESHAPE Plan

> Derived from the roast verdict (`./roast-verdict.md`, RESHAPE / high confidence) and the
> citation-verified storm-research briefing (`./dragoncandy-briefing.html`, 0 fabricated / 4 corrected /
> 4 demoted). This is not a pivot to something new — it refocuses DragonCandy around what's already
> half-built (DragonDash fulfillment + the Toast attribution rail) and buries what commoditizes (the
> marketplace framing, the four-fee stack, the AI-as-moat pitch).

## The one-line reshape

Stop being *"an AI creator–restaurant marketplace."* Become **"the done-for-you service that gets a
local restaurant proven diners from social — one price, measured in covers."** DragonDash *is* the
product; the marketplace, Donny, and the data flywheel are the engine room, never the pitch.

## Why (the diagnosis every lens converged on)

- Demand + willingness-to-pay are **real and correctly priced** ($149–899 sits inside a proven
  $500–5,000/mo restaurant social-content market — not a new behavior).
- But the **AI/matching layer is table stakes and commoditizing** (UGC prices −44% YoY, verified), and
  the marketplace layer is the one platforms absorb (YouTube shut FameBit's self-serve marketplace in
  2020; LivingSocial → $0; Groupon ~$13B → ~$1.1B by 2020).
- **The data-flywheel moat is misidentified** (this challenges our own stated "data flywheel is the
  primary moat"). Matching isn't the hard part; *measuring restaurant ROI* is — and the peer-reviewed
  literature has a genuine gap there (You et al. 2015: restaurants are the worst category for
  word-of-mouth; no causal study links creator content to actual covers).
- **Retention — not acquisition — is the whole game**, and SMB-restaurant churn is structurally brutal
  and front-loaded (~43% in the first 90 days; ~26% of restaurants close/change hands in a year).
- The four-fee stack + "60-second software" pitch + vanity (views) metrics are exactly the framing that
  historically dies.

## The seven shifts (from → to)

1. **Pricing: four fees → ONE flat outcome price.** Kill the *visible* stack. To the restaurant: one
   number (test **~$299–$499/mo** = N creator videos, posted, + a covers report). The creator payout,
   take-rate, and Donny's AI cost become **internal COGS**, invisible to the buyer. (See
   `./one-page-offer.md`.)
2. **Positioning: "60-second AI campaign" → "we get you diners, and prove it."** Lead with the outcome,
   not the mechanism. "Less typing = more margin" is an *internal* efficiency goal, not the sales line.
   **Bury Donny in the pitch** — AI brief+match+schedule is 2026 table stakes; it's how you keep cost
   down, not why they buy.
3. **Moat: data flywheel → attribution + system of record.** Build the one thing nobody has: POS-grade
   proof — *content → promo code/QR → Toast redemption → "this reel put N covers in seats."* Promote the
   Toast integration from a feature to the **spine**. Then get embedded (the ChowNow pattern — verified:
   flat-fee SaaS, restaurant owns its data): posting pipeline + creator relationships + performance data
   live inside DragonDash so leaving is operational surgery.
4. **Metric: views → covers/redemptions.** Every campaign ships with a trackable code and a *covers*
   report — never a views dashboard. This is what stops churn (retention = the entire LTV).
5. **Creator access: no gate-keeping — browse-and-pick, AI-match optional.** *(Updated per founder review,
   Joe Castelo, 2026-07-06 — see "Founder corrections" below.)* Let businesses **browse the full local
   creator roster** and pick who they want; that openness is a trust-builder, not a leak. AI-match (Donny)
   is an **optional shortcut** ("suggest one for me"), never a gate — DragonCandy must not hide creators
   from businesses. This is fully consistent with "done-for-you" (like a wedding planner who lets you pick
   the photographer from a vetted list, then handles everything else). Keep the reliability guarantee:
   whoever's picked shows up and delivers usable footage on deadline — fulfillment reliability is half the
   product.
6. **GTM: "launch when stable" → sell by hand NOW, one metro.** Stop treating launch as blocked on
   platform polish (the "building to avoid selling" tell). Hand-sign Hoboken restaurants + creators block
   by block. Creators-first stays right (supply-first sequencing is empirically validated), but recruit
   them around the *covers-proof* wedge, not "campaigns."
7. **Revenue: four streams on one SMB → flat fee now, brand-side later.** The defensible early money is
   the single managed-service fee. The brand/sponsorship dollars (the genuinely large outcome) come
   **after** a metro's audience + proven retention — don't lead with them, don't bank them in Y1.
8. **Product truth: REAL on-location footage is the core; AI-generated content is optional.** *(Founder
   correction, Joe Castelo.)* The heart of DragonCandy is a real creator filming the restaurant's *actual*
   food, kitchen, and vibe. AI-generated clips are an **opt-in add-on**, not the product. This is a
   *strength*, not a weakness: a robot can't film your real dining room — so "authentic footage of your
   real place, with proof it drove covers" is exactly what the cheap AI-content wave can't copy. (Trade-off
   to hold honestly: real on-site footage costs more to produce than an AI clip, so the flat price must
   cover a real creator's time — price accordingly.)

## Founder corrections (Joe Castelo review, 2026-07-06)

Two corrections from Joe — both adopted, both make the strategy stronger:

- **Don't gate-keep creators.** The original Shift #5 ("AI shortlists → owner approves") was too narrow.
  Businesses should be able to **browse every creator freely** and pick; AI-match is an *option*, not the
  only path. Openness/transparency is a trust and control feature — folded into Shift #5.
- **Real footage is the core; AI content is optional.** DragonCandy is real creators + real on-location
  footage — the authentic thing AI can't fake. This turns the earlier "AI-UGC will commoditize you" fear
  into a **moat**: real footage of the actual restaurant is the differentiated, defensible slice — added
  as Shift #8 and reflected in the risk below.

## Do this first — the 48-hour test

No product, no code. **One** Hoboken restaurant, one flat fee, 3 creator videos this week, a promo
code, measure redemptions. If an owner won't hand you cash for a tracked-covers outcome, the reshape's
core bet is wrong and you learned it for ~$0. Script: `./outreach-script.md`.

## Then the 90-day proof (the only number that matters)

Get ~5–10 restaurants onto the flat plan, delivered by hand (Donny/DragonDash behind the scenes to keep
cost low), each with a covers report. Watch **month-2 → month-3 resubscription.** Renew because they saw
diners → the model works, scale the playbook. Churn → no amount of AIOS/Donny machinery fixes it, and
you found out cheaply.

## What NOT to throw away

DragonDash fulfillment (that's the product now) · Donny + AIOS + the data logging (keep it — as the
internal cost/speed/ops **engine** and the margin edge, just not the pitch) · creators-first, one-metro
sequencing (validated) · the Toast integration (promote it to the attribution spine) · and keep logging
every campaign — the flywheel *does* compound later, it's just not what you sell or defend on **today**.

## The honest risk (the missing 6th lens: execution)

This reshape turns DragonCandy from a *software* company into a *services + measurement* company — a
different muscle than the (impressive) software already built. The failure mode isn't the strategy;
it's building more product while the winner is out hand-signing restaurants. On the AI-content wave:
because DragonCandy's core is **real, on-location footage of the actual restaurant** (Shift #8), it is
differentiated *against* commodity AI clips rather than replaced by them — the residual risk is market
*price confusion* (buyers seeing $0.60 AI videos and questioning your price), which the covers-proof and
authenticity answer. Build like the clock is running, but know real footage is your defense, not your
exposure.

## Bottom line

The wedge is fundable, the pain is real, and DragonCandy is closer to the winning version than the
current framing suggests. Sell one measured outcome as embedded infrastructure, and **prove one
restaurant renews before building anything else.**
