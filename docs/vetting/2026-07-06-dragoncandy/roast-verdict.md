# Roast Verdict — DragonCandy (solid & profitable?)

**The brief:** DragonCandy (dragoncandy.io) is an AI-powered creator–restaurant marketplace (HQ Hoboken, NJ) connecting Restaurants/Businesses, Content Creators, and Brands/Sponsors. Wedge = "DragonDash": rush, done-for-you short-form social content for local restaurants + SMBs, powered by "Donny," an AI layer that generates a campaign brief from a URL, matches creators, and cross-schedules posts (target: paid campaign in under 60s). Revenue stacks four ways on one customer: SaaS subscription ($149–$899), marketplace take-rate (10%→2%), Donny AI credit overages, and a DragonDash rush surcharge ($25–50). Pre-revenue by choice (~30 users, $0 paying, ~$450/mo burn), launching one metro at a time (Hoboken/NYC), creators-before-restaurants. Moat thesis: proprietary data flywheel + "#DragonDashed" verbification. Y1 goal: $300–600K ARR / 5–6 staff / 2–3 metros.

## THE VERDICT: RESHAPE
Confidence: high

**The call in one line:** The wedge is real and the market pays — but not for what you're currently selling or how you're pricing it. Reshape from "AI marketplace, four fees, 60-second software" into "one flat price, done-for-you local social, proven in covers."

**Why:** Demand and price band are validated; the pain is real. But four of five voices independently kill the same things — the four-fee stack, vanity metrics, and the "Donny-is-the-moat" story (it's table stakes). The bull case's flywheel moat is the least-supported claim in the room; the durable moat everyone else lands on is local creator density + reliable fulfillment per metro — the DragonDash service thesis, not the AI.

**Biggest risk:** Retention. Restaurant content ROI is unmeasurable-by-default, so if the owner can't feel or see diners from it, they churn in month two — and since retention is the entire LTV, weak felt-value collapses all four revenue streams at once.

**Biggest upside:** You're first to own restaurant-local creator supply in a metro nobody else targets that way; win Hoboken's supply side and the brand-side + Toast-distribution upside (the genuinely large outcome) opens downstream.

**Money read:** Pricing is right ($149–899 sits dead-center in a $500–5K/mo market — existing behavior, not new). Time-to-first-dollar could be days if sold manually as a flat-price managed service — the blocker is self-imposed (perfecting the platform). Y1 $300–600K = ~85–165 retaining accounts: achievable in count, gated entirely on felt-ROI retention.

**The cheapest 48-hour test:** No product. Get ONE Hoboken restaurant to pay a flat fee THIS WEEK for 3 creator videos with a trackable promo code / QR, and measure redemptions — not views. If you can't get one owner to hand you cash for a tracked-covers outcome in 48 hours, the AIOS/Donny/playbook machinery is answering a question the market hasn't been asked yet.

**If RESHAPE:** Kill the four-fee stack → ONE flat price tied to ONE measured outcome (covers/redemptions via Toast tickets). Reposition as a done-for-you managed service whose moat is local supply density + fulfillment (not the AI). Prove resubscription in ONE metro before spreading. Keep Donny as an internal cost/speed lever — not the pitch. The flywheel/brand/Toast upside comes later, earned by proven paid retention.

**Council scores:** Contrarian 3/10 · Expansionist 9/10 · Logician 6/10 · Researcher 6/10 · Buyer 4/10

## What the storm-research briefing changed

The deep, citation-verified briefing (5 lenses + primary-source verification: 0 fabricated, 4 corrected, 4 demoted) did **not** change the RESHAPE call — it **hardened** it and sharpened the pivot:

- **The bull's moat is directly refuted by the record.** The data/matching flywheel is exactly the layer that commoditizes (UGC prices −44% YoY, verified) and that platforms absorb (YouTube shut FameBit's self-serve marketplace in 2020; LivingSocial → $0; Groupon ~$13B → ~$1.1B by 2020, verified). The durable winners became embedded infrastructure the SMB can't rip out (ChowNow = flat-fee SaaS, 0% direct-order commission, restaurant owns its data — verified).
- **The retention risk now has a peer-reviewed root:** restaurants are the *worst* category for word-of-mouth elasticity (You et al. 2015, JM — volume .236 / valence .417, verified exact), and there is **no causal study** linking creator content to real restaurant covers (a genuine literature gap). So "prove it in covers" isn't just good hygiene — it's proving something the literature says is hard, which is precisely why building that measurement becomes the one moat that doesn't commoditize.
- **Sharpened pivot:** don't just "own local density" — become the **system of record** (own the posting pipeline + diner data) and **own the attribution layer** (POS/Toast match-back proving covers). Whoever owns restaurant-content ROI measurement owns the category.

Frontier question: *Can creator content be shown, at POS-grade rigor, to repeatably drive measurable covers at a price the owner pays monthly?* Answer it in one metro → there's a business; leave it unanswered → it's the Groupon/FameBit pattern with better tooling.
